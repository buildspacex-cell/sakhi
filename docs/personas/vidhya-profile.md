# Personal Profile: Vidhya

## Identity & Roots
I am a 43-year-old Indian-origin woman living in San Jose, California. I was born and raised in Chennai, Tamil Nadu. My upbringing was rooted in discipline, education, ambition, and strong family values.

I completed my engineering degree at SRM Engineering College in Chennai and moved to the United States in 2006 to pursue a Master's in Computer Science at the University of Kentucky. Before that, I worked at Tata Consultancy Services in India for about three years, including a year in the UK — my first exposure to living independently outside India.

Moving to the U.S. marked the beginning of building my life from scratch — professionally, emotionally, and financially.

## Professional Journey
I have built my career across technology and enterprise operations:

- Tata Consultancy Services (India & UK)
- Lexmark
- HP
- ACS (later acquired by Xerox and then Conduent)
- Automation Anywhere (current)

Today, I serve as VP of Corporate Operations at Automation Anywhere, working directly for the COO and founder. I operate effectively as his Chief of Staff and lead corporate operations — driving executive cadence, QBRs, company kickoffs, strategic initiatives, field enablement, and cross-functional alignment.

My boss is highly demanding and extremely hard to please. He equates long working hours with commitment and values visible intensity. The bar is always moving. Nothing is ever fully "done." The pressure is constant. I operate in a high-accountability, high-expectation environment that requires mental stamina and emotional restraint.

I am deeply self-driven. Achievement has always been a core part of my identity. I am a Type A personality — structured, perfection-oriented, analytical, and very outcome-focused. I do not like mediocrity in myself. I expect excellence.

At the same time, I am quietly reflective and sensitive underneath that high-functioning exterior.

## Marriage & Partnership
I am married to Dharma — my college sweetheart from India. We have grown up together emotionally. We built our early married life in Kentucky after our master's programs and later moved to New Jersey during our Conduent years before settling in California.

For most of our lives, we have been more friends and partners than anything else — steady, supportive, aligned. We have often worked in the same companies, including Conduent and now Automation Anywhere, which creates a unique overlap between personal and professional life.

## Children: The Center of My Emotional World
I have two children:

- A 17-year-old daughter (junior in high school)
- An 11-year-old son (sixth grade)

I am deeply attached to my children — emotionally invested in their happiness, identity, and success.

My daughter aspires to build a future in basketball and is exploring opportunities to play at Division II colleges. Basketball is not just a sport for her; it is part of her identity and confidence. I carry a very deep emotional bond with her. Her wins feel like my wins. Her struggles weigh on me. I often find myself internally replaying her games, her conversations, her future possibilities.

My son practices basketball as well but is also musically inclined. He has been learning piano for the past eight to nine months and shows natural rhythm and sensitivity. I watch him grow with curiosity — different from his sister, but equally strong in his own way.

Motherhood is not a role for me. It is an emotional anchor.

## Multi-Generational Living & Caregiving
I live with my parents, who are 76 and 74.

In April 2024, my father suffered a severe traumatic brain injury in an accident in our backyard. Before that day, he was energetic, disciplined, physically strong — my hero.

The accident resulted in:
- Skull fracture
- Multiple brain bleeds
- Long hospitalization and recovery

He survived, but life changed.

He is now a TBI patient. My mother is his primary caregiver. I see the emotional fatigue in her. I see the silent grief. I see the exhaustion.

While I am grateful to support them under my roof, the emotional load of watching my once-strong father in a vulnerable state has deeply altered me. It has changed my relationship with control, health, mortality, and time.

## Inner Life & Spirituality

Yoga gave me:
- Mental quiet

I crave that stillness again. I am actively trying to find the right timing to re-integrate it consistently into my life.

This entrepreneurial urge feels different from career ambition. It feels more like purpose.
